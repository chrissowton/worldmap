Which Countries?

What's new

Which Countries? is free and open source, and it keeps growing. These are the recent updates, newest first. Press Ctrl+Shift+R (⌘⇧R on a Mac) if the site doesn't show the latest version.

Version 70 November 2026

  • Future climate: how much warmer, and how much wetter or drier, every country is expected to be by 2050 and 2100, under low, middle and very high emissions. From the IPCC's AR6 Atlas (about 30 CMIP6 climate models). Find them under Planet → Climate, or as “Future warming” and “Future rainfall”.
  • Fixed: two CO₂ measures added in version 67 were duplicates of ones already on the site; they have been merged, so the originals now play year by year.

Version 69

  • Find a layer: type in More on the map (ice, war, trade, storms…) to find the layer you want, and press ☆ to keep favourites at the top.
  • Lesson starters: three questions at the bottom of every layer's box, with a button to copy them for a slide or worksheet.
  • Facts checked and updated for November 2026: the war with Iran and the Strait of Hormuz, the Houthis' control of Bab-el-Mandeb, new Colorado River rules, the Great Barrier Reef, and 2025 figures for every critical mineral (USGS 2026). Each layer's box says when it was checked.
  • On phones, the map moves up above an open box so you can still see the layer.
  • The site starts faster, especially on slower computers.

Version 68

  • Arctic sea ice (More on the map → Planet): the ice left each September in 1980, 1990, 2000, 2007, 2012, 2020 and 2026, with any year as an outline to compare, and a chart of every September since 1979 (NSIDC).
  • Colonial history (More on the map → World affairs): which European power ruled each of today's countries in any year from 1500 to 2000. Press play to watch empires grow and break up (COLDAT).
  • New measures: years under European colonial rule and the year it ended.

Version 67

  • China's overseas lending (More on the map → Global links): about 9,300 projects China financed abroad from 2000 to 2021, including the Belt and Road, by type, year and country (AidData).
  • Water: shared rivers: the Nile dam, the Mekong, the Indus, the Colorado, the Aral Sea and more, with examples of cooperation, and water stress for every country.
  • Fires now: fires spotted by NASA's satellites in the last 24 hours.
  • New climate measures: CO₂ in trade and total CO₂ emitted since 1750; CO₂ from what people buy and share of all CO₂ since 1750 can now be played year by year.

Version 66

  • Seven new layers in More on the map, each with a box that explains it and arrows to step through:
  • Plate movement: which way each tectonic plate moves and how fast, and arrows along the boundaries showing plates moving apart, together or sliding past.
  • Ocean currents: 27 warm and cold currents flowing round the gyres, each explained (climate, deserts, fog, fishing, El Niño).
  • Night-time lights: NASA's image of the Earth at night, with places to look at, from the two Koreas to the Nile.
  • Forest loss: deforestation hotspots and their causes, and the countries losing the most tree cover.
  • Undersea cables: the internet's 700 submarine cables, who owns them (including Google, Meta, Amazon and Microsoft) and where they crowd together.
  • World Heritage Sites: all 1,273, searchable, including the 2026 additions.
  • Critical minerals: who mines the lithium, cobalt, rare earths, nickel, copper, graphite and platinum needed for clean energy.
  • New measures: World Heritage Sites (every year since 1978) and international undersea cables.

Version 65

  • Cyclone tracks (More on the map → Planet): the path of every hurricane, typhoon and cyclone of category 3 or more since 1980, about 1,100 storms, coloured by strength. Choose a decade, a strength or an ocean, see the strongest, and click a track to follow one storm as it grows and fades. Data: IBTrACS (NOAA).
  • Protected areas: two dozen of the biggest and best-known national parks and marine reserves, each explained, with the “30 by 30” target.
  • New measure: protected sea (marine protected areas), and protected land updated to 2025. Goal 14, Life below water, now shows in each country's Sustainable Development Goals.

Version 64

  • Case studies: pins for 27 GCSE and A-level case studies (earthquakes, storms, floods, cities, ecosystems, UK landscapes), each with key facts, links to the right maps, and arrows to step through them. Also in Exam topics.
  • Shipping lanes and chokepoints: the world's main shipping routes, the busiest ports, and 11 chokepoints from Suez to Hormuz, each explained.
  • More on the map is now a library of themes that fold open, showing what's switched on, with a note if the map gets busy.
  • When several boxes are open on the map they become tabs, and each box scrolls inside itself (no more stray scroll bars).

Version 63

  • Step through conflicts and disputes from west to east, and elections in date order, with ◀ and ▶.
  • Zooming and dragging the map works again when boxes are open on it.
  • With two or more number filters, choose which one colours the map: click 🎨 on a filter, or pick it in the colour key.

Version 62

  • World affairs in More on the map: current conflicts (from the Global Conflict Tracker), disputed borders and territories (Kashmir, Western Sahara, Taiwan, Crimea and 20 more), and elections coming up and in the last year, each with a short explainer.
  • More on the map opens fully on screen, with a fuller explanation of each layer when you hover over it.
  • The list can show countries, countries and territories, or territories only.

Version 61

  • The colour key is now a box on the map (bottom left), with buttons to colour the map by any of your other filters. Notes about unusual years appear there too, so the year player never changes size.
  • Filter lists show the main three in each group, with “＋ more” for the rest.
  • Filters spin in, with a ping, when added, and spin out when removed (turn the sound off with 🔔 by “Filters”).
  • Hover over “Map style: Landforms” (and the other styles) to see what it means.

Version 60

  • With three or more filters, the sentence above the map is short (“Showing 54 countries that match all 4 filters”), with “Read them in full” to open it.
  • Show every filter in the list: a column for each filter, with each country's figure and a ✓ or ✗, so you can see why a country is in, or which filter keeps a near miss out.

Version 59

  • The map's extra layers (regions, city growth, climate graphs, live hazards) are in one menu, More on the map, grouped and explained, next to Map style.
  • Each tool's window has its own colour, and the one you click comes to the front.
  • Factfile, Side by side and Number hunt open as windows over the map, which you can resize or make full screen.
  • Statistics starts with the measures you used last.
  • Build your own index shows each measure in its topic colour.
  • Table view has been removed (the list on the right does the same job).

Version 58

  • A clearer year player: a bigger, coloured timeline with decades marked, a large year, and a layout that stays still whatever the notes say.
  • With several yearly filters, the averages sit in one tidy line, and “All” lists every one.

Version 57

  • Income mountains (Charts → Income mountains): how many people live on each income, stacked by region, from 1800 to 2040, with the extreme poverty line and Gapminder's four income levels. Press play to watch the world move out of poverty.
  • How certain are population projections? Charts of population, life expectancy and fertility (with projections) now show the UN's 80% and 95% ranges as shaded bands for any country you add, and for the world and continents' population.
  • UN population projections now draw as smooth lines with world and continent totals.

Version 56

  • Regions within countries: the 🗺️ Regions button colours about 1,800 regions in 188 countries by the Human Development Index (every year from 1990), life expectancy, schooling, income and gender measures, with figures for women and men. See the highest and lowest regions, the countries with the biggest gaps between regions, every region of one country, and one region in detail. Data: Global Data Lab.
  • Faster start: the main data starts downloading straight away, while the rest of the page loads.
  • This page.

Version 55

  • Small multiples: one small chart for each continent or country, side by side.
  • Worldview quiz: questions about the world that most people get wrong, worked out from the latest data.
  • Describe now works for every chart, not just the map.
  • Notes: draw arrows, circles, highlights and text on any chart or map before downloading it.
  • Questions for students: teachers can add up to five questions to a shared link; students answer on the page.
  • Dollar Street links for every country.

Version 54

  • About this data for every chart and the map: definitions, sources, licences, years, coverage and how to cite.
  • A page for every measure, with every country ranked.
  • Embed any chart or map in a website or Google Site.
  • Line charts can show the change since a start year (or % change, or an index).
  • Bar chart race for any yearly measure.

Versions 50–53

  • Every chart has Download and Full screen buttons; country names or codes can be shown on charts.
  • The bubble chart fits the countries shown, has a title, a trend line and an option to show only matching countries.
  • Clearer filter editor (Add this filter / Done), colour-coded sections, resizable pop-ups, map boxes in the bottom-left corner, and side columns that close.
  • Statistics: plain-words summaries, explanations for every figure, sortable tables, and amber and red for spread that stands out.
  • Your own index can be used in every chart.

Versions 44–49

  • Climate graphs for thousands of weather stations, migration flows, city growth from 1975 to 2050, and about 50 new measures (corruption, press freedom, conflict deaths, child marriage, tourism, energy and more).
  • Quiz maker for teachers, Describe this map, a statistics toolkit (Spearman's rank, chi-squared, Lorenz curves), exam topics, guided tours, live hazards, a colour-blind mode, keyboard use and a table view.
